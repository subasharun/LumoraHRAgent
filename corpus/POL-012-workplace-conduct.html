Workplace Conduct and Anti-Harassment Policy

Lumora Systems, Inc. is a fictional company. This document is synthetic and was written for an AI engineering course project.

1. Our Commitment

Lumora is committed to a workplace where everyone is treated with dignity and respect. We do not tolerate harassment, discrimination, bullying, or retaliation of any kind. This policy applies to all employees, interns, and contractors, and to conduct with customers, vendors, and visitors. It covers behavior in offices, remote meetings, chat and email, Company events, business travel, and social media where the conduct affects the workplace.

2. Expected Conduct

Everyone at Lumora is expected to:

  • treat colleagues, customers, and partners with respect and courtesy;
  • communicate professionally in all channels, including chat and video calls;
  • avoid conflicts of interest and disclose any potential conflict to their manager and Legal;
  • protect Company and customer information (see POL-006);
  • comply with all laws and Company policies;
  • speak up when they see behavior that violates this policy.

3. Prohibited Conduct

3.1 Discrimination

Lumora prohibits discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, pregnancy, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, national origin, age, disability, genetic information, veteran or military status, or any other characteristic protected by law. This applies to hiring, pay, promotion, assignments, discipline, termination, and all other terms of employment.

3.2 Harassment

Harassment is unwelcome conduct related to a protected characteristic that creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment, or where submission to the conduct is made a condition of employment. Examples include slurs, offensive jokes, threats, intimidation, unwelcome physical contact, and displaying offensive images.

3.3 Sexual harassment

Sexual harassment includes unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal, written, or physical conduct of a sexual nature. It can occur between people of any gender and regardless of seniority.

3.4 Bullying

Bullying is repeated, unreasonable behavior directed at a person that creates a risk to health and safety, such as persistent belittling, exclusion, or public humiliation. Reasonable management actions, such as giving performance feedback in a respectful manner, are not bullying.

3.5 Workplace violence

Threats of violence, violent acts, and possession of weapons on Company premises or at Company events are prohibited. In an emergency or immediate danger, call local emergency services first (911 in the US and Canada, 999 in the UK), then notify Security.

3.6 Retaliation

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith, participates in an investigation, or requests an accommodation or leave is strictly prohibited and is itself a serious violation of this policy, regardless of the outcome of the original report.

4. How to Report a Concern

Employees can report concerns through any of these channels. You do not have to report to your manager first, and you should not report to anyone involved in the concern.

  • Your manager or any other manager you trust.
  • People Operations, through an HR ticket in the "Employee Relations" category. These tickets are visible only to the Employee Relations team.
  • The confidential Ethics Hotline, available 24/7 by phone and web, which accepts anonymous reports where local law allows.
  • Legal, for concerns involving fraud, bribery, or legal compliance.

Managers who receive a report must forward it to Employee Relations within 1 business day, even if the employee asks them to keep it confidential. Managers must not investigate on their own.

Automated tools, including the HR assistant, can explain this policy and help an employee open an Employee Relations case, but cannot investigate, make findings, or advise on the merits of a specific complaint.

5. Investigation Process

  1. Employee Relations acknowledges the report within 1 business day.
  2. An investigator is assigned and begins the investigation within 5 business days. Investigators are trained and have no reporting relationship with the people involved.
  3. Interim measures may be put in place to protect the people involved, such as schedule changes or a temporary change of reporting line. Interim measures must not disadvantage the person who reported.
  4. The investigator interviews the people involved and witnesses and reviews relevant documents.
  5. Most investigations are completed within 30 calendar days. The reporting employee and the respondent are told when the investigation is complete and, where appropriate, whether the policy was found to be violated.

Information is shared only with those who need it to investigate and act. Confidentiality is maintained to the extent possible, but complete anonymity cannot always be guaranteed.

6. Consequences

Violations may lead to corrective action, including training, a written warning, removal of management responsibilities, or termination of employment or contract. Knowingly making a false report is also a violation, but a good-faith report that is not substantiated is never a violation.

7. Relationships at Work

Romantic or sexual relationships between a manager and anyone in their reporting chain are prohibited. Other workplace relationships must be disclosed to People Operations if they could create a conflict of interest.

8. Support

Anyone affected by a workplace conduct issue may use the Employee Assistance Program (see POL-008, Section 7.2) for free, confidential counseling.

9. Training

All employees complete Workplace Conduct and Anti-Harassment training within 14 days of hire and every year after. Managers complete additional training within 30 days of becoming a manager.
