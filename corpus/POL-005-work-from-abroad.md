# Work From Abroad and Cross-Border Work Policy

> Lumora Systems, Inc. is a fictional company. This document is synthetic and was written for an AI engineering course project.

## 1. Purpose

Working from another country, even for a short time, can create obligations that most people do not expect: personal income tax in the other country, social security contributions, immigration and right-to-work requirements, data protection rules, and "permanent establishment" risk for the Company (where a country treats the Company as doing business there and taxes it accordingly). This policy creates a simple, safe path for short periods of work from abroad and explains when a full Global Mobility review is required.

This policy applies only to work performed from a country **other than** the country of the employee's registered work location. Temporary work from another state or province inside the home country is covered by the Remote and Hybrid Work Policy (POL-004, Section 4).

## 2. Definitions

- **Work From Abroad (WFA):** A short, pre-approved period during which an employee works remotely from a country other than their registered work location country, while remaining employed and paid in their home country.
- **Workday abroad:** Any business day on which the employee performs any work (including checking email or attending a meeting) while physically located outside their registered work location country. Weekends and company holidays of the registered work location do not count as workdays, unless the employee works on them.
- **Rolling 12 months:** The 12-month period ending on the last day of the requested WFA period.
- **Global Mobility review:** A formal assessment of tax, payroll, immigration, and legal requirements for longer or more complex cross-border work, conducted by the Global Mobility team with external advisors.

## 3. Eligibility

To use the WFA program, an employee must:

1. be a full-time or part-time employee (interns and contractors are not eligible; see POL-001);
2. have completed the 90-day introductory period;
3. hold a remote-eligible role (POL-004, Section 3.1);
4. be a citizen of, or already hold the legal right to work in, the destination country **or** be traveling to a country where Global Mobility has confirmed that short remote work is permitted for visitors of the employee's nationality; and
5. not be traveling to a restricted country (Section 6).

## 4. Duration Limits

| Workdays abroad in a rolling 12 months | Program | Approval required |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 20 workdays | Work From Abroad (WFA) | Manager + Global Mobility + Information Security |
| More than 20 workdays | Not permitted under WFA | Requires a Global Mobility review; may only proceed as a formal international assignment or a permanent relocation |

**Twenty workdays is approximately four calendar weeks.** A request for six weeks of work abroad (about 30 workdays) exceeds the WFA limit and cannot be approved under the WFA program. The employee may either (a) shorten the work period to 20 workdays or fewer, (b) combine a shorter work period with PTO, since days on approved PTO are not workdays, or (c) request a Global Mobility review by opening an HR ticket in the "Global Mobility" category. Global Mobility reviews typically take **6 to 8 weeks**, and most requests for extended work abroad that are not tied to a business need are declined.

A single WFA period may not exceed 20 workdays, and the total across all WFA periods in any rolling 12 months may not exceed 20 workdays.

## 5. How to Request Work From Abroad

1. Confirm eligibility under Section 3 and check that the destination is not restricted (Section 6).
2. Discuss the dates with your manager.
3. Submit a **Work From Abroad** request in PeopleHub at least **30 calendar days** before departure. Include destination country, city, dates, and citizenship or visa status.
4. Global Mobility reviews tax and immigration risk and responds within 10 business days.
5. Information Security confirms device and access requirements (Section 7) and may issue a travel loaner device.
6. Do not perform any work abroad until PeopleHub shows all three approvals as complete.

Employees who travel abroad on PTO and do no work at all do not need a WFA request, but must follow the device rules in Section 7 if they bring a Lumora device.

## 6. Restricted Countries

Work, including checking email, is not permitted from the following countries and regions under any circumstances, because of sanctions, export control, or data security risk: **Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Russia, Belarus, and the Crimea, Donetsk, and Luhansk regions of Ukraine.** Lumora devices must not be taken to these locations. Information Security may add other destinations to a "high-risk" list, for which a travel loaner device is mandatory.

## 7. Security Requirements While Abroad

While working abroad, employees must:

- use only their Lumora-issued device or a travel loaner issued by IT;
- connect through the Company VPN at all times;
- not access Restricted data (POL-006, Section 3) from outside their home country unless Information Security has approved it in writing;
- never leave devices unattended in hotel rooms, vehicles, or public places;
- report a lost or stolen device to the IT Service Desk within 1 hour of discovery (POL-006, Section 9).

Access to some customer systems is blocked from outside the employee's home country by contractual requirements. Employees should confirm with their manager that they can do their full job from the destination before requesting WFA.

## 8. Tax, Payroll, and Immigration

During an approved WFA period, the employee remains on their home-country payroll. Lumora does not provide tax advice to employees about their personal tax obligations; employees should consult a personal tax advisor. Employees are responsible for having the correct passport, visa, and entry documents. Entering a country as a tourist and then working there can violate immigration law even if the work is for a foreign employer; Global Mobility will flag destinations where this is a concern.

## 9. Work Hours and Holidays

Employees must remain reachable during their team's core collaboration hours (POL-004, Section 5), which may mean working early or late in the destination time zone. The holiday calendar of the registered work location applies (POL-003, Section 7).

## 10. Expenses

Lumora does not reimburse travel, accommodation, or living costs for personal WFA periods. WFA is a benefit of flexibility, not business travel. Business travel is governed by the Expense Reimbursement Policy (POL-007).

## 11. Non-Compliance

Working abroad without approval, exceeding the approved dates, or working from a restricted country may result in loss of WFA eligibility, disciplinary action, and the employee being responsible for any resulting personal tax liabilities.

## 12. Frequently Asked Questions

**I want to work from Portugal for six weeks this summer. Can I?** Not under the WFA program. Six weeks is about 30 workdays, which exceeds the 20-workday limit. You could work from Portugal for up to 20 workdays and take PTO for the remainder, or request a Global Mobility review through an HR ticket at least 8 weeks in advance.

**Does checking email on vacation count as a workday?** Yes. Any work performed while abroad counts. If you are on PTO abroad, do not work.

**I am a Canadian citizen working for the Atlanta office. Can I work from Canada?** Your citizenship may satisfy the right-to-work requirement, but tax and payroll obligations still apply. You must submit a WFA request; the 20-workday limit applies.
