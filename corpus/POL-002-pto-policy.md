# Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy

> Lumora Systems, Inc. is a fictional company. This document is synthetic and was written for an AI engineering course project.

## 1. Purpose

Lumora believes that rest is essential to doing good work. This policy explains how eligible employees earn paid time off (PTO), how to request it, how much notice is required, and how unused PTO is handled. PTO covers vacation, personal days, and other planned time away. Sick leave, bereavement leave, jury duty, and parental leave are separate and are described in the Leave of Absence Policy (POL-009). Company holidays are described in the Holiday Calendar (POL-003) and do not reduce an employee's PTO balance.

## 2. Eligibility

Full-time and part-time employees accrue PTO as described in Section 3. Interns and contractors do not accrue PTO (see Employment Classifications, POL-001). Employees in their 90-day introductory period accrue PTO from day one, but may use no more than 3 days of PTO before day 90 unless their manager and People Operations approve an exception.

## 3. Accrual Rates

PTO accrues each pay period (semi-monthly, 24 pay periods per year) based on tenure:

| Tenure | Annual PTO (full-time) | Accrual per pay period |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 2 years | 15 days (120 hours) | 0.625 days (5.0 hours) |
| 2 years to less than 5 years | 20 days (160 hours) | 0.833 days (6.67 hours) |
| 5 years or more | 25 days (200 hours) | 1.042 days (8.33 hours) |

Part-time employees accrue PTO at the full-time rate multiplied by their FTE. For example, a part-time employee with an FTE of 0.6 and less than two years of tenure accrues 9 days per year.

The higher accrual rate begins on the first pay period after the employee reaches the relevant work anniversary.

### 3.1 Balance Cap

An employee's PTO balance may not exceed **30 days (240 hours)** for full-time employees, prorated for part-time employees. When the balance reaches the cap, accrual pauses until PTO is used and the balance drops below the cap. PeopleHub displays a warning when an employee is within 5 days of the cap.

### 3.2 Carryover

Up to **5 days (40 hours)** of unused PTO may be carried over into the next calendar year. Any balance above 5 days on December 31 is forfeited, except where local law requires otherwise. In Canada and the United Kingdom, statutory minimum vacation entitlements are always preserved and may not be forfeited; see the country supplements maintained by People Operations.

## 4. Requesting PTO

All PTO must be requested in PeopleHub and approved by the employee's direct manager before the time off begins. Verbal or chat approval is not sufficient. When submitting a request, employees should:

1. Check their available balance in PeopleHub, including any pending requests that have not yet been deducted.
2. Confirm that the dates do not overlap with company holidays (holidays do not need to be requested and do not use PTO).
3. Submit the request with the required notice described in Section 5.
4. Arrange coverage for critical responsibilities and set an out-of-office message.

Managers must approve or decline PTO requests within **3 business days**. If a manager does not respond within 3 business days, the employee may escalate to their skip-level manager or open an HR ticket in the "Time Off" category.

### 4.1 Approval Requirements

- Requests of **1 to 4 consecutive business days** require direct manager approval.
- Requests of **5 to 10 consecutive business days** require direct manager approval and the employee must confirm coverage for any on-call rotation.
- Requests of **more than 10 consecutive business days** require direct manager approval **and** department director approval.

Managers may decline a request when it conflicts with critical business needs, such as a product launch, customer commitment, or quarter-end close, but should work with the employee to find alternative dates. A manager may not decline a request for a discriminatory or retaliatory reason.

## 5. Notice Requirements

Employees must give advance notice based on the length of the request:

| Length of request | Minimum notice |
|---|---|
| 1 business day | 2 business days |
| 2 to 4 business days | 5 business days |
| 5 to 10 business days | 10 business days |
| More than 10 business days | 20 business days |

Notice is measured in business days from the date the request is submitted in PeopleHub to the first day of the requested time off. Company holidays and weekends do not count as business days.

### 5.1 Short-Notice Requests

A manager may approve a request submitted with less than the minimum notice at their discretion. Short-notice requests are not guaranteed. Employees should explain the reason for the short notice in the request comments. Emergencies and illness should use sick leave or the appropriate leave type under POL-009 instead of PTO.

## 6. Negative Balances and Advances

Employees may not take PTO that they have not yet accrued, with one exception: full-time employees with at least one year of tenure may request an advance of up to **3 days** against future accrual, with manager approval. An advance is repaid automatically from future accruals. Employees may not have more than one outstanding advance.

## 7. Blackout Periods

Some teams designate blackout periods during which PTO requests are generally not approved, such as the last two weeks of a fiscal quarter for Finance or major release windows for Engineering. Blackout periods must be published at least 60 days in advance in the team calendar. Requests that were approved before a blackout period was published will be honored.

## 8. PTO and Holidays

If a company holiday falls within an approved PTO period, the holiday is not deducted from the employee's PTO balance. For employees working outside their home country under an approved work-from-abroad arrangement (POL-005), the holiday calendar of their registered work location applies.

## 9. PTO at Termination

Upon separation, accrued and unused PTO (up to the balance cap) is paid out in the final paycheck where required by law or where the employee is in a jurisdiction that requires payout. In other US states, Lumora pays out accrued, unused PTO as a matter of policy. Any outstanding PTO advance is deducted from the final paycheck where permitted by law.

## 10. Recording Time Off

Exempt employees record full days of PTO only; partial days under 4 hours are not recorded. Non-exempt employees record PTO in hourly increments, with a minimum of 1 hour.

## 11. Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I take PTO during my first 90 days?** Yes, up to 3 days, with manager approval. More than 3 days requires HR approval as well.

**What happens if I am sick during approved PTO?** If you are sick for 2 or more days during approved PTO, you may ask your manager to convert those days to sick leave. The PTO days are then restored to your balance.

**Can my manager require me to take PTO?** Managers may encourage employees with balances near the cap to schedule time off, but may not require an employee to use PTO except during a company-wide closure announced at least 90 days in advance.

**Who do I contact if my balance looks wrong?** Open an HR ticket in the "Time Off" category with the dates in question. People Operations will respond within 3 business days.
